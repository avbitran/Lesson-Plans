# Past Simple vs. Past Progressive: Review

| | |
|---|---|
| **Class** | 10th grade, 5-point (5 יח"ל) English |
| **Learners** | Native Hebrew speakers, roughly CEFR B1+ to B2 |
| **Length** | 45 minutes, plus homework |
| **Type** | Review lesson. Students have already learned both tenses, so the aim is accurate use, not first exposure |

## Objectives

By the end of the lesson students will be able to:

1. Pick the right tense: Past Simple for completed actions and sequences, Past Progressive for background and actions in progress at a past moment.
2. Use **when** and **while** correctly to show one action interrupting another.
3. Spot and fix the past-tense errors Hebrew speakers typically make.
4. Use both tenses in speech (Alibi game) and in writing (homework narrative).

## Why Hebrew speakers struggle with this

Hebrew has **one past tense** and no grammatical progressive aspect. Both *I studied* and *I was studying* are **למדתי**. As a result, students:

- **Overuse Past Simple** where English needs the progressive: *"At 8 o'clock I ate dinner"* when they mean "was eating".
- **Mix the forms**: *"I was go"*, *"When I was arrive"*, because they treat *was* as a past-tense marker.
- **Put the past into the main verb twice**: *"I didn't went"*, *"Did you saw?"*
- **Use the progressive with stative verbs**: *"I was knowing"*, *"She was wanting"*.
- **Skip question inversion**: *"Where you were going?"*, which copies Hebrew word order (איפה הלכת?).
- **Pronounce -ed as an extra syllable**: *"work-ed"*, *"play-ed"*.

The lesson tackles these directly in Stage 3 (error clinic) instead of hoping they disappear.

## Stage 1: Warm-up (5 min)

**Aim:** get both tenses out of students' mouths before any explanation, and show you whether they mix them up.

1. Write on the board: **Yesterday at 8:00 p.m.**
2. Ask 3–4 students: *"What were you doing yesterday at 8 p.m.?"* Write their answers on the board exactly as said, **errors included**. Don't correct yet.
3. Follow up with: *"And what happened next?"* / *"Did anything interrupt you?"*
4. Pairs ask each other the same question about 7:00 a.m. this morning and 10:00 p.m. last night (2 min).

Keep the sentences on the board. You'll come back to them at the end of Stage 3.

## Stage 2: Guided discovery (10 min)

**Aim:** students work out the rules themselves from a text instead of being told.

1. Students read *The Night the Lights Went Out* (handout, Part A) once for general meaning. Ask one gist question: *"Why does the writer remember that night?"* (1 min)
2. Individually, students underline Past Simple verbs in **blue** and Past Progressive verbs in **red** (3 min).
3. In pairs, they answer the discovery questions in Part B (4 min).
4. Whole-class check. Build this table on the board from what students say:

| Past Simple | Past Progressive |
|---|---|
| Completed action at a specific past time | Action **in progress** at a past moment |
| A **sequence** of events (first → then → next) | **Background** and scene-setting in a story |
| The **short** action that interrupts | The **longer** action that gets interrupted |
| Stative verbs (*know, want, like, have* = possess, *believe*) | Two long actions happening at the same time |

**Signal words:**

- **when** + Past Simple (the interruption): *I was studying **when** the lights **went** out.*
- **while** + Past Progressive (the long action): ***While** Noa **was looking** for a flashlight, I opened the window.*

**The Hebrew point (spend 1 minute on it):** ask *"How do you say 'I was studying' and 'I studied' in Hebrew?"* Both are למדתי. Then make it explicit: **English forces you to choose, Hebrew doesn't.** Whenever students write a past verb in English, they need to ask: *was it finished, or was it in the middle?*

### Answer key: Part B

1. **Past Simple:** went (out), screamed, stopped, went (silent), opened, noticed, had (light), didn't know, found out, had (a generator), came back, didn't do, learned. **Past Progressive:** was studying, was raining, was blowing, was watching, were cooking, was looking, were standing (and shouting), was sitting (and playing), was working, weren't studying, were singing.
   *Note: in "were standing and shouting" and "was sitting and playing", the second -ing verb shares the same was/were. Point this out, because students often repeat it ("was sitting and was playing"), which is correct but clumsy.*
2. Past Progressive. It describes the scene and background.
3. Past Simple. They happened one after another, as a sequence of complete actions.
4. Looking for a flashlight was the longer action. Opening the window happened during it.
5. *Know* is a stative verb (a state, not an action), so it isn't normally used in progressive forms.
6. Both are **למדתי**. Hebrew doesn't show the difference, so English speakers have to choose deliberately.

## Stage 3: Error clinic (7 min)

**Aim:** target typical Hebrew-speaker errors head-on.

1. Pairs correct the 8 sentences in Part C (4 min). Tell them each sentence has **exactly one** problem, though the fix may change more than one word.
2. Whole-class check (2 min). For each one, ask *"Why was it wrong?"* and get a reason, not just the corrected sentence.
3. Return to the warm-up sentences on the board. Ask the class to correct any errors there (1 min).

### Answer key: Part C

| # | Error | Correction | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | When I was arrive home, my mother cooked dinner. | When I **arrived** home, my mother **was cooking** dinner. | *was* + base form doesn't exist. Arriving interrupts the cooking. |
| 2 | I didn't went to school yesterday. | I didn't **go**… | After *did / didn't*, use the base form. The past is already in *did*. |
| 3 | Where you were going when I saw you? | Where **were you** going…? | Questions need inversion: *was/were* comes before the subject. |
| 4 | While I watched TV, the phone was ringing. | While I **was watching** TV, the phone **rang**. | The tenses are reversed. The long action is progressive, the interruption is simple. |
| 5 | She was knowing the answer. | She **knew** the answer. | *Know* is stative. |
| 6 | They was playing football. | They **were** playing… | Subject–verb agreement: *they were*. |
| 7 | Did you saw the match? | Did you **see**…? | Base form after *did*. |
| 8 | At 8 o'clock I ate dinner, so I didn't answer the phone. | At 8 o'clock I **was eating** dinner… | The action was in progress at that moment. |

**Optional pronunciation minute:** if students say "work-ed" during the check, drill the three -ed sounds: /t/ *worked, watched, stopped* · /d/ *played, screamed, opened* · /ɪd/ *wanted, started, needed*. The rule: /ɪd/ comes only after a **t** or **d** sound.

## Stage 4: Controlled practice (8 min)

**Aim:** accurate form under light time pressure.

1. Part D1: gap-fill story, 10 gaps (5 min).
2. Part D2: join the sentences with *when* or *while*, 4 items (3 min).

Students check answers in pairs while you walk around. Only go through as a class the items that more than a few students got wrong.

### Answer key: Part D1

1. was walking
2. started
3. didn't have
4. ran
5. were sitting
6. was talking
7. saw
8. smiled
9. were you doing
10. didn't know

### Answer key: Part D2 (more than one correct version is possible)

1. I was taking a shower **when** the doorbell rang. / **While** I was taking a shower, the doorbell rang.
2. **While** Dan was driving to Eilat, his phone fell under the seat. / Dan was driving to Eilat **when** his phone fell under the seat.
3. **While** we were playing basketball, it started to rain. / We were playing basketball **when** it started to rain.
4. **While** my dad was cooking, my sister was doing her homework. (Two long actions at the same time, so both are progressive.)

## Stage 5: Freer practice: Alibi (12 min)

**Aim:** use both tenses in spontaneous speech, with a real reason to be accurate (if your answers don't match, you lose).

**Scenario (read aloud):**
> "Yesterday between 7:00 and 9:00 p.m., someone ate the entire birthday cake in the teachers' room. Nobody saw who did it. You are all suspects."

**Setup:**

- Split the class into **suspect pairs** and a group of **3–4 detectives** (for a large class, run two detective groups).
- **Prep (3 min):** suspect pairs agree on a detailed alibi. They were together all evening, so they need matching details: where they were, what they were doing at 7:15, 7:45, 8:30, what the weather was like, what they ate, what happened and what interrupted them. They may **not** write the alibi down.
- Meanwhile, detectives prepare questions using the question bank (Part E).
- **Interrogation (6 min):** detectives question each suspect **separately**, about 1 minute each. Detectives note every detail that doesn't match.
- **Verdict (3 min):** detectives announce the pair with the most mismatches. That pair is "guilty". Use the last minute for **delayed error correction**: write 3–4 errors you overheard on the board, anonymously, and have the class fix them.

**What to listen for while monitoring:**
- *was + base form* ("I was go")
- Past Simple used for a moment in progress ("At 7:15 we watched a movie")
- missing inversion in detective questions ("What you were doing?")

**Small class (under 12):** you play the detective and every pair is a suspect.

## Stage 6: Exit ticket and homework (3 min)

**Exit ticket (Part F).** Students hand it in on the way out. Use it to decide who needs a second look at the forms next lesson.

### Answer key: Part F

1. was reading, heard
2. b. *While I was waiting for the bus, I saw an accident.*
3. Free answer. It must contain one correct Past Progressive and one correct Past Simple, joined by *when* or *while*.

### Homework: narrative paragraph (Bagrut writing practice)

> **Write about a time when something unexpected happened to you or someone you know (120–150 words).**
> Your text must include:
> - at least **4** Past Progressive verbs
> - at least **6** Past Simple verbs
> - **when** and **while**, at least once each
> - a sentence that sets the scene at the start (what was happening when the story began?)
>
> Underline the Past Progressive verbs once and the Past Simple verbs twice.

**Quick marking guide (out of 10):**

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---|
| Correct tense choice (simple vs. progressive) | 4 |
| Correct forms (irregular verbs, *did* + base form, *was/were* agreement) | 3 |
| *When / while* used correctly | 1 |
| Clear narrative with a background → event → outcome structure | 2 |

## Differentiation

**Support (weaker students):**
- Give them a list of 20 common irregular verbs (go–went, see–saw, find–found, come–came, etc.) for Stages 4–6.
- Sentence frames for the Alibi game: *"At ___ we were ___ing ___. Then ___ happened."*
- In the error clinic, let them do sentences 1–6 only.

**Challenge (stronger students):**
- In the homework, add **"just as"** and **"as"** (*Just as I was leaving, the phone rang*) and at least one Past Perfect for an earlier event (*we found out that he **had bought** a generator*). This previews the next tense contrast.
- During discovery, ask: *"'Later, we found out that he had a generator.' Could this also be 'had bought'? What changes?"*

## Possible follow-up lesson

Past Simple vs. Past Perfect, or *used to / would* for past habits. The second also deals with mistakes like *"I was go to the beach every summer"*, where students use *was* as a general "past" marker. That comes from the same Hebrew-interference source.
