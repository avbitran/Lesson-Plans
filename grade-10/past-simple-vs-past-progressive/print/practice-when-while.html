when or while?

NameClassDate
while + long action

While I was walking home, I met Noa.

While needs something that lasts.

when + short action

I was walking home when it started to rain.

When also works with a long action.

two long actions

While I was cooking, my sister was studying.

Both progressive; while is best.

Watch out: "While I've done my homework…" is wrong. I've done is the Present Perfect (finished, connected to now). In a story about the past, use I was doing.

#AnswerWhy
1when"The phone rang" is a short action. While can't introduce a moment.
2both"We were eating" is long, so when and while both work.
3whenThe film ending happens in a moment.
4both"She was cooking" is long.
5whenHearing the news is a moment, and the two actions happen one after the other.
6both · while is betterTwo long actions at the same time. When is correct but less natural.

Past Simple or Past Progressive?

#AnswerWhy
1was waiting, startedwhile + long action; the rain starting interrupts
2cut, was makingCutting happened in the middle of making. Cut is irregular: cut–cut–cut
3was playing, was readingTwo long actions at the same time
4saw, screamed, ranAll Past Simple: a sequence, one action after another. When + Past Simple in both clauses = "first this, then that"
5rang, was takingThe ring interrupts the test

Item 4 is the trap: students who think "when always means interruption" write was screaming. Use it to show that when + Past Simple + Past Simple means one action after another.

Your turn: what to check

  • a The when clause holds a short action in the Past Simple, e.g. I was sleeping when my alarm went off.
  • b The while clause holds a long action in the Past Progressive, e.g. While I was waiting for the bus, I saw my teacher.
  • c Both verbs are in the Past Progressive, ideally with while, e.g. While I was cooking, my brother was setting the table.

Common slips to look for: was + base form ("I was go"), while + a short action, and the Present Perfect used for a story ("While I've…").
