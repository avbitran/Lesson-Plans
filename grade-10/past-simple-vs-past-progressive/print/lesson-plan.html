Past Simple vs. Past Progressive

Finished, or in the middle? A review lesson built around Hebrew speakers' typical mistakes.

Class
10th grade, 5-point (5 יח"ל)
Learners
Hebrew speakers, CEFR B1+ to B2
Length
45 minutes + homework
Type
Review: accuracy, not first exposure

Objectives

By the end of the lesson students will be able to:

  1. Pick the right tense: Past Simple for completed actions and sequences, Past Progressive for background and actions in progress.
  2. Use when and while to show one action interrupting another.
  3. Spot and fix the past-tense mistakes Hebrew speakers typically make.
  4. Use both tenses in speech (Alibi) and writing (homework).

Materials

  • Student handout, one per student
  • Slide deck (optional)
  • Two colours of marker: blue = Past Simple, orange or red = Past Progressive
  • A timer for the Alibi game

Why Hebrew speakers struggle

Hebrew has one past tense and no progressive aspect: I studied and I was studying are both למדתי. So students:

  • Overuse Past Simple for actions in progress: "At 8 o'clock I ate dinner."
  • Mix the forms: "I was go", treating was as a past marker.
  • Mark the past twice: "I didn't went", "Did you saw?"
  • Add -ing to stative verbs: "I was knowing".
  • Skip inversion: "Where you were going?" (Hebrew word order).
  • Say -ed as a syllable: "work-ed".

Stage 3 (error clinic) tackles these head-on.

1

Warm-up: "What were you doing…?"

5 min · whole class → pairs

Get both tenses out of students' mouths before any explanation, and see who mixes them up.

  1. Write on the board: Yesterday at 8:00 p.m.
  2. Ask 3–4 students: "What were you doing yesterday at 8 p.m.?" Write their answers on the board exactly as said, mistakes included. Don't correct yet.
  3. Follow up: "And what happened next?" / "Did anything interrupt you?"
  4. Pairs ask each other about 7:00 this morning and 10:00 last night (2 min).

Keep the board sentences. You'll come back to them at the end of Stage 3.

2

Guided discovery: The Night the Lights Went Out

10 min · individual → pairs

Students work out the rules from a text instead of being told.

  1. Students read the story (handout Part A) once. Gist question: "Why does the writer remember that night?" (1 min)
  2. Individually, they underline Past Simple in blue and Past Progressive in orange (3 min).
  3. In pairs, they answer the discovery questions in Part B (4 min).
  4. Whole-class check. Build the rules table on the board from what students say (2 min).
Past Simple
  • Completed action at a specific past time
  • A sequence of events (first → then → next)
  • The short action that interrupts
  • Stative verbs: know, want, like, have (= own), believe
Past Progressive
  • Action in progress at a past moment
  • Background and scene-setting in a story
  • The longer action that gets interrupted
  • Two long actions at the same time

Signal words: when + Past Simple: I was studying when the lights went out. · while + Past Progressive: While Noa was looking for a flashlight, I opened the window.

The Hebrew point (1 minute): ask how to say "I was studying" and "I studied" in Hebrew. Both are למדתי. Make it explicit: English forces you to choose, Hebrew doesn't. Every past verb in English needs the question: finished, or in the middle?

Answer key · Part B
  1. Past Simple: went (out), screamed, stopped, went (silent), opened, noticed, had (light), didn't know, found out, had (a generator), came back, didn't do, learned.
    Past Progressive: was studying, was raining, was blowing, was watching, were cooking, was looking, were standing (and shouting), was sitting (and playing), was working, weren't studying, were singing.
    In "were standing and shouting", the second -ing verb shares the same were. Students often repeat it; that's correct but clumsy.
  2. Past Progressive. It describes the scene and background.
  3. Past Simple. One after another, a sequence of complete actions.
  4. Looking for the flashlight was longer. Opening the window happened during it.
  5. Know is a stative verb (a state, not an action), so it doesn't normally take -ing.
  6. Both are למדתי. Hebrew doesn't show the difference, so English speakers have to choose deliberately.
3

Error clinic

7 min · pairs → whole class

Target typical Hebrew-speaker errors head-on.

  1. Pairs correct the 8 sentences in Part C (4 min). Each has exactly one problem, though the fix may change more than one word.
  2. Whole-class check (2 min). For each one, ask "Why was it wrong?" and get a reason, not just the correction.
  3. Go back to the warm-up sentences on the board and correct those too (1 min).
Answer key · Part C
#MistakeCorrectionWhy
1When I was arrive home, my mother cooked dinner.When I arrived home, my mother was cooking dinner.was + base form doesn't exist. Arriving interrupts the cooking.
2I didn't went to school yesterday.I didn't go…After did / didn't, use the base form.
3Where you were going when I saw you?Where were you going…?Questions need inversion.
4While I watched TV, the phone was ringing.While I was watching TV, the phone rang.Tenses swapped: long = progressive.
5She was knowing the answer.She knew the answer.Know is stative.
6They was playing football.They were playing…Agreement: they were.
7Did you saw the match?Did you see…?Base form after did.
8At 8 o'clock I ate dinner, so I didn't answer the phone.At 8 o'clock I was eating dinner…In progress at that moment.

Optional pronunciation minute: if students say "work-ed", drill the three -ed sounds: /t/ worked, watched, stopped · /d/ played, screamed, opened · /ɪd/ wanted, started, needed. /ɪd/ comes only after a t or d sound.

4

Controlled practice

8 min · individual

Accurate form under light time pressure.

  1. Part D1: gap-fill story, 10 gaps (5 min).
  2. Part D2: join the sentences with when or while, 4 items (3 min).

Students check in pairs while you walk around. Go through as a class only the items more than a few students got wrong.

Answer key · D1
  1. was walking
  2. started
  3. didn't have
  4. ran
  5. were sitting
  6. was talking
  7. saw
  8. smiled
  9. were you doing
  10. didn't know
Answer key · D2 (other orders are fine)
  1. I was taking a shower when the doorbell rang.
  2. While Dan was driving to Eilat, his phone fell under the seat.
  3. We were playing basketball when it started to rain.
  4. While my dad was cooking, my sister was doing her homework. (Two long actions, both progressive.)
5

Freer practice: Alibi

12 min · groups

Both tenses in spontaneous speech, with a real reason to be accurate: if your answers don't match, you lose.

Scenario (read aloud)

"Yesterday between 7:00 and 9:00 p.m., someone ate the entire birthday cake in the teachers' room. Nobody saw who did it. You are all suspects."

  • Split the class into suspect pairs and 3–4 detectives (two detective groups for a big class).
  • Prep (3 min): suspects agree on a detailed shared alibi: where they were and what they were doing at 7:15, 7:45 and 8:30, the weather, what they ate, what interrupted them. Nothing written down. Detectives prepare questions from Part E.
  • Interrogation (6 min): detectives question each suspect separately, about 1 minute each, and note every detail that doesn't match.
  • Verdict (3 min): the pair with the most mismatches is "guilty". Spend the last minute on delayed error correction: write 3–4 errors you overheard on the board, anonymously, and have the class fix them.

Listen for: was + base form ("I was go") · Past Simple for a moment in progress ("At 7:15 we watched a movie") · missing inversion ("What you were doing?").
Small class (under 12): you play the detective and every pair is a suspect.

6

Exit ticket and homework

3 min · individual

Students hand in the exit ticket (Part F, cut-off slip) on the way out. Use it to decide who needs a second look at the forms next lesson.

Answer key · Part F
  1. was reading, heard
  2. b. While I was waiting for the bus, I saw an accident.
  3. Free answer: one correct Past Progressive and one correct Past Simple, joined by when or while.

Homework: narrative paragraph

Write about a time when something unexpected happened to you or someone you know (120–150 words). Include at least 4 Past Progressive and 6 Past Simple verbs, when and while once each, and a scene-setting opening sentence. Underline Past Progressive once, Past Simple twice.

Differentiation and follow-up

Support
  • A list of 20 common irregular verbs (go–went, see–saw, find–found…) for Stages 4–6.
  • Sentence frames for Alibi: "At ___ we were ___ing ___. Then ___ happened."
  • Error clinic: sentences 1–6 only.
Challenge
  • Homework: add just as / as (Just as I was leaving, the phone rang) and one Past Perfect for an earlier event.
  • Discovery extra: "he had a generator" vs. "he had bought a generator". What changes?

Next lesson: Past Simple vs. Past Perfect, or used to / would for past habits. The second also catches "I was go to the beach every summer", the same "was = past" interference.
